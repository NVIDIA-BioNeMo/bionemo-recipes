    PhiX174 screening rationale and evidence

    Supporting evidence for the current recipe results. The publication cohort below is selected; it is not a raw-generation baseline.

    ARC selected-cohort waterfall (2026-10-19)

    Applying our current v3 screen to ARC's 302 ordered records leaves two designs, both labeled nonviable in Science Data S1. The records contain 296 exact-unique sequences; the waterfall below counts each sequence once. This is attrition within an already selected publication cohort, not ARC's generation pass rate or a controlled comparison of RL and SFT. In production rollouts, raw-observation yields remain separate from final duplicate removal and 99%-identity clustering.

    Largest filter exclusions

    These are additional exclusions in the sequential filtering order, ranked by count. They are not independent failure rates; changing gate order can change the attribution.

    Filter Additional unique designs excluded
    Length 98
    Nine-core completeness 90
    G-protein identity + coverage 49
    Circular-ORF AAI 43
    ARC count OR structured K+X diversification 13
    Core order/copies 1
    Sequence safety 0

    What drives core-completeness exclusions?

    Among the 149 designs reaching the core-completeness gate:

    Core assessment Unique designs Gate outcome
    At least one required family not detected 61 Excluded
    All nine families detected, but at least one fails completeness/coverage 29 Excluded
    All nine core functions complete 59 Retained

    The first two categories account for all 90 core-completeness exclusions. These are annotation-based assessments, not proof of biological absence. The subsequent order/copy gate excludes one extra-core-copy design; no additional order violation was observed among these 59.

    Supplemental: Full sequential waterfall and attribution notes

    Sequential gate Unique designs remaining Additional exclusions Viable-labeled designs remaining*
    Ordered cohort, exact-unique sequences 296 — 16
    Length 5,306–5,730 nt 198 98 15
    G-protein identity ≥60%, with ≥95% query and target coverage 149 49 15
    All nine core functions meet completeness criteria 59 90 15
    Core circular order and no extra full-coverage core copies 58 1 15
    Sequence-safety PASS 58 0 15
    Circular-ORF AAI ≤95% 15 43 1
    Original ARC count diversification OR structured K+X add-one-gene route 2 13 0
    Final duplicate removal / 99%-identity clustering 2 0 0

    The unchanged nucleotide-character, GC, homopolymer, ORF and other configured preliminary checks remove no additional candidates at their positions in this saved run. The independent original-ARC checks, including its seven-protein-hit requirement, remove none of the 15 candidates entering the final module before diversification. The added K+X route contributes zero ARC designs. The standalone architecture-diversification cutoff is disabled.

    Which filters dominate? In this execution order, length removes 98, followed by core completeness removing 90. Of those core failures, 61 lack at least one detected required family; 29 have all nine families detected but fail at least one full-length/coverage criterion. These are annotation-based failures, not proof that a gene is biologically absent. The later order/copy gate rejects one extra-core-copy case, with no additional order violation among its 59 inputs. Safety passes all 296 unique inputs independently. Measured-only AAI rejects 106 of 198 length-qualified designs, but only 43 additional designs after the preceding gates. Later metrics were not measured on every early rejection: their marginal counts cannot be compared using a common 296 denominator or summed as disjoint losses.

    The G-stage loss is also coverage-sensitive: identity alone accepts 195 of its 198 inputs; requiring a qualifying hit with ≥95% coverage of both query and reference leaves 149. This coverage requirement is our added constraint; ARC's published description specifies ≥60% identity. It must not be described as 49 failures of ARC's identity threshold or of the seven-hit gate.

    Why our length window differs from ARC's

    ARC's published Design constraints section specifies 4–6 kb, and the released pipeline template sets genome_length_range: [4000, 6000]. All 296 unique ordered sequences fit that range. Our retained PhiX profile instead uses 5,306–5,730 nt in both the hard nucleotide gate and final screening. This is an additional project-specific constraint, not an equivalent implementation of ARC's length criterion. See the Science supplement, p. 4 and released configuration.

    • Lower bound, 5,306 nt: inherited from an earlier narrow PhiX profile whose upper bound was 5,493 nt. The introducing configuration cites a local 155-sequence PhiX/Sinsheimervirus collection spanning 5,339–5,388 nt, but does not derive the exact 5,306 cutoff. It is 80 nt below the 5,386-nt reference. Treat it as a heuristic guard against truncation, not an experimentally established minimum viable genome length; its precise justification remains weak.
    • Upper bound, 5,730 nt: the maintained configuration cites engineered PhiX174 insertion mutants recovered at that length. Russell and Müller reported these mutants, while noting instability and selection of deletions above approximately 5,550 nt. This supports a cautious screening choice, not a universal packaging ceiling for diversified genomes. See Russell and Müller, 1984.
    • These hard bounds are separate from the graded length reward's four-point envelope. The observed lengths of recovered viable phages and the lengths of originally ordered designs are also different evidence and should not be substituted for each other.

    Our window rejects 40 short designs (4,117–5,275 nt) and 58 long designs (5,737–5,998 nt). Counting ordered records instead gives 41 short and 59 long, because some sequences were ordered more than once. One excluded long design is 5,948 nt: batch_2_evo1_microviridae_ft_5k_temp07_topk4_prompt4bp_genome_3392. Its publication label is “viable, acquired mutation(s) during rebooting.” That supports success from this nominated design lineage, not viability of the unchanged 5,948-nt input. No length threshold was changed for this audit.

    Evidence for shorter genomes and ARC boot outcomes

    Shorter-than-wild-type PhiX174 genomes have experimental support, but the endpoint matters:

    • Small deletions in phage genomes: Russell and Müller (1984) cite Müller and Wells' earlier J–F intergenic-region work. Incardona and Müller (1985) directly studied DNA ejection by phage particles carrying a 27-base deletion in this region, corresponding to approximately 5,359 nt relative to the 5,386-nt reference. A later H-protein study recovered viable progeny retaining a seven-amino-acid deletion after a compensatory mutation. These demonstrate tolerance of particular small deletions, not a whole-genome minimum. Russell and Müller references; Incardona and Müller, 1985; H-protein length study, 2016.
    • Much shorter packaged DNA: Aoyama and Hayashi (1985) reported efficient production of infectious particles with DNA 78.5–101% of wild-type length in an in-vitro packaging system. The lower endpoint is approximately 4,228 nt, calculated from 5,386 nt. These were plasmid-derived templates with phage machinery supplied separately; this does not demonstrate a complete, independently propagating phage at 4.23 kb. Aoyama and Hayashi, 1985.

    All 16 ARC designs labeled viable had original input lengths above our 5,306-nt minimum. This includes nine labeled viable and seven labeled viable with mutations acquired during rebooting. The shortest successful input was 5,349 nt (99.31% of wild-type length): batch_3_evo2_microviridae_ft_12k_temp09_topk4_prompt9bp_genome_36. It is labeled viable, without the acquired-mutations qualifier. These are original ordered-design lengths and publication outcome labels, not lengths inferred for recovered mutant genomes.

    Original design length Exact-unique designs Failed DNA synthesis Tested designs Viable-labeled
    Below 5,306 nt 40 2 38 0
    5,306–5,385 nt (shorter than wild type) 22 0 22 1

    Counts use the deduplicated Science Data S1 outcome audit; synthesis failures are excluded from the tested denominator. The 40 nominated short inputs span 4,117–5,275 nt, and there are no ordered designs at 5,276–5,305 nt. Thus the cohort is consistent with a conservative lower screen, but cannot establish length as the cause of failure or validate the precise 5,306-nt boundary. That boundary is approximately 98.5% of wild-type length, or an 80-nt allowance; the numerical relationship does not establish its original derivation.

    Experimental outcomes and interpretation

    A separate diagnostic relaxed only the length window for the excluded 5,948-nt design and measured all nine core functions as complete, all nine in expected circular order, and zero extra core copies. Its exact sequence also has safety PASS in the fully reconciled original cohort. Thus all 16 viable-labeled designs pass core+safety when evaluated independently of the length gate (nine labeled viable as designed; seven with mutations acquired during rebooting). The production waterfall still excludes the long design before core evaluation. This diagnostic does not change the length cutoff or the production acceptance counts.

    *The viable-labeled column includes nine designs labeled viable and seven labeled viable with mutations acquired during rebooting. The two final passers are batch_3_evo1_microviridae_ft_5k_temp09_topk4_prompt11bp_genome_66 and batch_3_evo1_microviridae_ft_5k_temp11_topk4_prompt4bp_genome_3; both are labeled nonviable. Fifteen viable-labeled designs survive core and safety; AAI novelty removes 14, and the final diversification intersection removes the remaining one. These novelty constraints are not viability predictors.

    ARC applied diversification routes separately and manually selected candidates. Our combined screen requires AAI plus a diversification route and extra core/safety constraints; v3 also adds the independently evidenced K+X alternative. Consequently, these endpoints do not establish a simple strict-subset relationship. A design failing our combined screen could still be eligible for ARC's AAI-only selection route. Circular-AAI correction and original-inventory AAI diagnostics remain explicitly distinguished in the final-filter contract.

    Evidence for core conservation among AAI-diverse designs

    To ask whether core conservation helps retain bootable, AAI-diverse candidates, compare designs passing circular-ORF AAI ≤95% with the same length and G-protein gates, without requiring either original ARC count diversification or the structured K+X alternative. This is a retrospective comparison within ARC's selected cohort, using exact-unique ordered sequences.

    Core+safety outcome among AAI passers Successfully synthesized/tested designs Viable-labeled Nonviable
    Pass nine-core completeness, order/copies and safety 15 1 14
    Fail core completeness 48 0 48

    One additional core-incomplete AAI passer failed DNA synthesis and is excluded from the experimental denominators above. All designs in this comparison pass safety. Among these AAI passers, the completeness gate accounts for all 49 computational exclusions; the subsequent order/copy checks remove no additional designs. This evidence therefore supports the more stringent core gene set/completeness requirement, but does not independently demonstrate an incremental benefit from the order/copy checks or safety. Order/copy checks remain explicit biological constraints rather than an experimentally isolated benefit in this comparison.

    Core filtering retains the sole viable-labeled AAI passer while excluding 48 nonviable designs. The observed viable-labeled fraction rises from 1/63 (1.6%) before core filtering to 1/15 (6.7%) afterward. The successful lineage is batch_3_evo2_microviridae_ft_12k_temp09_topk4_prompt9bp_genome_5, labeled viable with mutations acquired during rebooting. Neither group contains a design demonstrated to boot unchanged.

    This is suggestive support for retaining the stringent core filter, not a validated estimate of enrichment or a causal claim: there is only one positive outcome, the cohort was already selected by ARC, and acquired mutations separate the nominated input from the successful phage. Fourteen core-complete AAI passers were still nonviable. Larger prospective experimental testing is needed to establish whether the filter improves boot rates for newly generated diverse designs.

    These counts are derived by joining the saved length, G, required-gene, core-order/copy, circular-AAI and safety outcomes to Science Data S1 labels by exact sequence hash. They do not substitute the original ARC-inventory AAI diagnostic for our explicitly versioned circular-AAI gate.
